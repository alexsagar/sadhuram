# Design Principles

Every design, engineering, and architectural decision in this portfolio must adhere to twelve foundational principles:

### 1. Subject Before Spectacle
The subject is Er. Sadhuram Lamichhane's authoritative expertise in GIS, Geomatics, Earth Observation, and Spatial Planning. Technology, 3D WebGL, and animation exist strictly to illuminate that subject. If a visual effect distracts from the subject, it must be removed.

### 2. Content Determines Layout
Layout is not an arbitrary decorative container into which content is poured. The nature of geospatial deliverables—orthomosaics, hazard models, cadastral maps, technical tables, and academic citations—dictates the visual structure.

### 3. Restraint Creates Authority
True engineering authority is calm and self-evident. It does not shout, flash, or demand attention through gimmicks. Deep confidence is communicated through deliberate whitespace, meticulous typography, and disciplined restraint.

### 4. Motion Communicates Structure
Motion is an instrument of spatial explanation, not entertainment. Animations reveal layer relationships, demonstrate transitions from terrain to cadastral data, and guide the eye along analytical pathways.

### 5. Real Evidence Beats Decoration
Replace decorative icons, stock photos, and abstract vector shapes with authentic project outputs: survey benchmarks, satellite imagery, slope maps, elevation profiles, and documented municipal plans.

### 6. No Component-Library Aesthetic
Avoid standard boilerplate components (e.g., standard shadcn cards with identical borders, radius, and drop shadows). Every component must feel purpose-crafted for geospatial editorial publishing.

### 7. Every Breakpoint Is Intentionally Designed
Mobile is not a squeezed desktop view. Small screens receive tailored compositions, optimized 3D geometry or static fallbacks, and re-architected typography to preserve dignity and legibility.

### 8. Performance Is Visual Quality
A sluggish, stuttering 3D scene destroys professional credibility immediately. Smooth frame rates (60fps), lightweight asset footprints, adaptive device pixel ratios (DPR), and clean hydration are non-negotiable visual standards.

### 9. Typography Carries Hierarchy
Typographic scale, tracking, line height, and contrast must establish unambiguous reading order. Headings are measured, body copy is comfortable for long reading, and metadata (coordinates, dates, scales) is precisely keyed.

### 10. Whitespace Is Structural
Whitespace is treated as active structural margin—allowing complex spatial graphics and technical data to breathe without cognitive overload.

### 11. Complexity Belongs in Data, Not UI Chrome
Let the underlying spatial data and analytical findings be rich and nuanced. Keep the UI chrome (navigation, buttons, borders, containers) minimal, crisp, and unobtrusive.

### 12. Every Visual Choice Needs a Reason
No element, border, line, color accent, or animation shall exist simply because an AI assistant generates it by default. Every visual choice must answer: *Why is this here, and how does it advance the geospatial narrative?*
